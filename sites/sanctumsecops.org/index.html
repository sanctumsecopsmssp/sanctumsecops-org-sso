Sanctum Timestamp is an open, OpenTimestamps-compatible proof-of-existence service under active development. The design: hash a file in your browser, commit the digest to a public append-only Merkle log, and keep a portable receipt that anyone can verify without asking us for permission — or trusting us at all.

The open log answers when. Where that is not enough — regulated evidence, contract execution, chain-of-custody, release integrity — an attested proof will bind a hardware-held identity to the same commitment and answer who.

Two questions, two different proofs

Most timestamping services answer only the first. Conflating them is why evidence gets challenged.

Open tier · free

When did this data exist?

Your digest is aggregated into a Merkle tree and committed to the public log. The inclusion proof plus the signed tree head establish that the data existed no later than that commitment. This is a mathematical claim. It does not depend on our continued goodwill, our uptime, or our honesty — a third party can recompute it from the receipt alone.

Answers: existence, ordering, and integrity. Says nothing about: authorship.

Attested tier · paid

Who asserted it, and under what authority?

An enrolled identity will sign the same commitment with a private key that lives on a YubiKey in PIV slot 9c and cannot be exported. The receipt will carry the PIV attestation certificate chain, so a verifier can establish that the signature came from that hardware token, held by that verified identity, at that moment. This tier is not yet available — hardware signing is still in development.

Adds: non-repudiable authorship bound to hardware, plus the organizational identity proof collected at enrollment.

How a proof is made

Your file is never uploaded. Only a 32-byte digest crosses the network.

  1. Hash locally

    SHA-256 is computed in your browser with WebCrypto. The file itself never leaves your device, so the service never sees your content and cannot leak it.

  2. Submit the commitment not deployed

    The digest will be posted to /log/append. Submissions inside the current aggregation window are batched into one Merkle tree. No endpoint is serving this domain yet.

  3. Sign the tree head

    The new root is published as a signed tree head. The signature is composite — ECDSA P-256 and ML-DSA-65 — and a tree head is only valid when both legs verify, so the log stays verifiable after a classical break.

  4. Attest, if the tier calls for it not available

    For attested proofs the enrolled YubiKey signs the leaf, and the PIV attestation chain is embedded in the receipt. Hardware signing currently fails closed rather than emit an unattested receipt.

  5. Take your receipt and leave

    You receive a portable receipt containing the digest, the inclusion path, the signed tree head, and any attestation. Verification needs no account and no call home.

Terminal — stamp and verify (illustrative)
# Digest never leaves your machine unless you send it
$ sha256sum contract-v4.pdf
9f2b41c8…  contract-v4.pdf

# Commit the digest to the open log
$ sanctum stamp contract-v4.pdf
  digest   sha256:9f2b41c8…
  leaf     1284417
  tree     size=1284418 root=3ae0…
  receipt  contract-v4.pdf.ots

# Anyone can verify — no account, no network call to us
$ sanctum verify contract-v4.pdf.ots
  inclusion    OK  path len 21
  sth ecdsa    OK
  sth ml-dsa   OK
  attested     no  (open tier)
  existed by   2026-08-27T18:04:11Z

What a timestamp is not. A proof of existence attests that data existed at a point in time. It does not make the contents true, lawful, accurate, or authorized. Anyone claiming otherwise is overselling the primitive.

Why the proof survives us

A trust service you have to trust is not a trust service.

01 · Independence

Verification is offline

A receipt is self-contained. Recomputing the Merkle path and checking the tree-head signature requires no API call to us. If this service disappeared tomorrow, existing receipts remain verifiable against published tree heads.

02 · Append-only

The log cannot be rewritten

Consistency proofs let any auditor confirm that a newer tree head is a strict extension of an older one. Retroactively removing or reordering an entry breaks consistency publicly and permanently.

03 · Post-quantum

Dual-leg tree heads

Tree heads are signed with a composite scheme. A valid signature requires both the classical ECDSA leg and the ML-DSA-65 leg to verify, so a future break of one does not retroactively invalidate the log.

04 · Hardware root

Keys that cannot be copied planned

Attested identities will sign from a YubiKey PIV slot. The private key is generated on the token and is not exportable, so an attested signature cannot be produced by exfiltrated key material. Not yet in production.

05 · Interoperable

OpenTimestamps compatibility planned

Receipts are designed to follow the OpenTimestamps receipt structure so existing tooling can consume them — you are not buying into a format only we can read. The .ots serialiser is not yet implemented.

06 · Content-blind

We never hold your data

Only digests are submitted. There is no content to subpoena, breach, or mishandle — the strongest privacy posture available for this class of service.

Where attested proofs earn their price

The free tier is genuinely sufficient for most uses. These are the cases where it is not.

Use caseWhat breaks with a bare timestampWhat attestation adds
Regulated evidence An auditor accepts that the record existed, then asks who produced it. A digest cannot say. A named, hardware-bound signer per record, with the enrollment identity proof retained.
Contract execution Both parties can prove a document existed. Neither can prove the other assented. Counter-signed commitments, each bound to a distinct token.
Software release integrity Proves the artifact existed — including one an attacker built. Only an enrolled release token can produce a valid attested proof.
Chain of custody Establishes ordering but not custody. Handoffs are invisible. Each transfer is signed by the receiving party's token, producing an attributable chain.
Federal and defense work No link between the evidence and a verified organizational identity. Enrollment verified against CAGE, so the signer maps to a registered entity.
Research precedence Proves a result existed by a date; a rival can claim they held the same digest. Binds the claim to an identity and, optionally, an ORCID.
