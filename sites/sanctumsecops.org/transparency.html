Log design · RFC 6962 lineage

An append-only log
you can audit against us

The log is a Merkle tree over submitted digests. Its useful property is not secrecy or immutability by assertion — it is that misbehaviour by the operator is detectable by anyone holding two tree heads. This page describes exactly what is published and what a watcher can catch.

Construction

ElementDefinitionWhy
Leaf hash SHA-256(0x00 || digest) The 0x00 domain-separation prefix prevents a leaf from being reinterpreted as an interior node — the second-preimage attack RFC 6962 exists to close.
Interior node SHA-256(0x01 || left || right) Distinct prefix from leaves, so tree shape cannot be forged by substitution.
Root Fold of all leaves at the current tree size A single 32-byte commitment to the entire log contents and ordering.
Signed tree head {tree_size, root_hash, timestamp}, composite-signed Converts a hash into an operator commitment. Publishing two different roots for the same tree size is undeniable, self-incriminating evidence.
Inclusion proof Sibling hashes from a leaf to the root Proves one digest is in the tree, in log₂(n) hashes, without revealing or transferring the rest of the log.
Consistency proof Proof that tree n is a prefix of tree m Proves nothing was removed, reordered, or rewritten between two tree heads. This is what makes "append-only" checkable rather than promised.

The composite signature

Both legs must verify. One valid leg is a verification failure, not a partial pass.

Classical leg

ECDSA P-256

Universally supported, verifiable today by any standard library, in any language, with no special tooling. This leg is what lets a verifier in 2026 check a tree head using software they already trust.

Post-quantum leg

ML-DSA-65 (FIPS 204)

A lattice signature standardised by NIST and selected under CNSA 2.0. This leg is what keeps historic tree heads meaningful after a cryptographically relevant quantum computer makes the ECDSA leg forgeable.

Why timestamping specifically needs this. Most protocols can rotate to post-quantum algorithms and move on, because they only protect live sessions. A timestamp makes a claim about the past that must still hold decades later. If tree heads were signed with ECDSA alone, then the day P-256 falls, every historical proof becomes forgeable and the entire log's evidentiary value evaporates retroactively. Dual-leg signing is not future-proofing — for this primitive it is the product.

What a watcher can detect

Independent monitoring is welcome and unmetered. Please break it.

Detectable

A split view

Two validly signed tree heads at the same tree size with different roots proves the operator served different histories to different parties. Both artifacts are signed, so the evidence is self-proving.

Detectable

A rewritten history

If a later tree head fails a consistency proof against an earlier one, entries were removed or reordered. Anyone who retained an old tree head can demonstrate it.

Detectable

A withheld entry

A submitter holding an append acknowledgement whose digest never appears in a subsequent tree can show the log accepted and then omitted it.

Detectable

A downgraded signature

A tree head carrying only the ECDSA leg, or a malformed ML-DSA leg, must be rejected. Anti-downgrade at verification is mandatory, not advisory.

Not prevented

A refused submission

Availability is not a cryptographic property. The log can decline or delay a submission. Cross-anchoring to an independent log is the mitigation, and it is why the free tier stays OpenTimestamps-compatible.

Not claimed

Truth of contents

The log commits to a digest. It makes no claim that the underlying data is accurate, lawful, or authorised. Attestation adds authorship, never veracity.
