Pricing · effective 2026

The proof is free.
The identity is the product.

Open timestamping stays free and unmetered, permanently and without registration. That is not a loss leader gesture — an open log is only credible if anyone can use and audit it, and its value grows with volume it does not bill for.

Revenue comes from the layer above: binding a verified, hardware-held identity to a commitment, and from the operational surfaces organizations need around that — automation, retention, auditor access, and sovereign deployment.

Why the model is shaped this way

The pricing follows from the trust properties, not from what the market tolerates.

Free tier

The open log must be free to be credible

A transparency log's value is a function of how many independent parties use and watch it. Metering submissions would suppress exactly the volume that makes the log worth trusting, and would invite the reasonable objection that the operator can price people out of auditing it. Free also removes the account requirement, which removes us as a gatekeeper.

Per signer, not per proof

Identity is a standing relationship

The costly, defensible work is verifying an organization, provisioning non-exportable hardware, and maintaining a revocable registry. That cost is per identity and recurs annually, so the price is per signer. Metering attested receipts on top of that would discourage the thorough evidence practice the product exists to enable.

Verification is never billed

Charging the verifier breaks the primitive

If a verifier must pay us to check a proof, the proof depends on us — which is the exact property the service claims to eliminate. Auditor seats, the verification portal, and offline verification are free at every tier, permanently.

Compliance premium

You are buying the audit outcome

The Compliance tier's cost is not cryptography. It is retention obligation, control mapping, export tooling, and a named human who will answer an assessor's questions. That is what the price reflects, and it is priced against the cost of failing an assessment, not against the cost of a hash.

Sovereign licensing

Some buyers cannot use a shared log

Classified-adjacent and sovereignty-constrained programs cannot submit digests to an externally operated service, however content-blind. Licensing the instance captures that demand without weakening the public log, and cross-anchoring keeps the two compatible.

Federal track

CAGE verification is the differentiator

Mapping a signer to a registered entity via CAGE is a control almost no commercial timestamping service offers. It is also the requirement that makes the proof usable in a procurement or contract-dispute context, which is where the budget lives.
